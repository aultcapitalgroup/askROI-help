When askROI retrieves information from the web or analyzes external content, it doesn't just hand you an answer — it shows you exactly where that answer came from. Every factual response includes numbered citations linked to the original sources, giving you a clear trail from claim to evidence.

## Why Citations Matter

Reliable research depends on verifiable sources. Without citations, there is no way to judge whether an AI's answer is accurate, current, or drawn from a credible authority. askROI builds citations into every web-based response so you can evaluate the quality of each source, spot potential bias, and confirm that the information holds up before you act on it or share it with others.

## How Citations Work

Whenever askROI performs a web search or cites an external fact, it appends numbered reference markers — like `[1]`, `[2]` — directly inline with the relevant sentence or claim. At the end of the response, a **Sources** section lists each reference with:

- The **title** of the page or article
- The **domain** it comes from (e.g., `reuters.com`, `pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov`)
- A short **excerpt** from the source so you can preview relevance before clicking

This format keeps the main answer readable while making the evidence easy to inspect at any time.

## Verifying Sources

To verify any citation, click its reference number or title in the Sources section. The original page opens in a new browser tab so you can read the full context yourself. If you want to dig deeper into a specific source, paste the URL back into the askROI chat — askROI can analyze the page, extract key passages, or help you understand technical content.

When working on high-stakes research, cross-reference the most important claims against at least two independent sources. askROI's web search retrieves multiple results per query, so you can ask follow-up questions to surface additional perspectives.

<Tip>
  askROI is a powerful research tool, but AI systems can make mistakes — including citing a source that doesn't fully support the claim it's attached to. Always click through to verify critical facts, especially for medical, legal, financial, or safety-related decisions.
</Tip>
